Is offline processing safer than uploading?

Uploading is harmless for a public meme, and unacceptable for the documents most people actually need to compress or convert:

  • Contracts and legal paperwork. Client agreements and signed documents are confidential by definition; a compression tool has no business keeping a copy.
  • Medical, financial, and HR records. In many jurisdictions (HIPAA, GDPR) passing them through a third-party server can create compliance exposure you never agreed to.
  • Client work under NDA. "We delete files after one hour" is a policy, not a guarantee, and it is theirs to change.
  • Bulk photo archives. Uploading gigabytes of personal photos to convert them to WebP wastes time and bandwidth you did not need to spend.

Verdict: for anything private, offline wins: nothing is uploaded, so nothing can be retained, leaked, or read.

Is local processing slower than a data center?

A data center computes fast, but the bottleneck is not the computing: it is the round-trip. A 50 MB scan takes seconds to upload on a good connection and minutes on a bad one, then the result travels back. Processing the same file locally takes about a second and starts the moment you drop it.

Verdict: for typical files, local wins on speed: the upload never happens, and neither does the queue.
